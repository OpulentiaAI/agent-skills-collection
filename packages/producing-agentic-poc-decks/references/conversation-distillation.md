# Conversation Distillation

This file captures the reusable lessons from the Codex deck-building thread and the attached Claude strategy thread.

## What The User Was Really Building

The artifact was not merely a deck. It was a sales narrative for getting Farnsworth to commit to a paid POC that would become an SOW.

The deck had to:

- explain the operational pain in Farnsworth's current process;
- respect the existing custom GPT while showing why it was not enough;
- connect fraud, bad actors, and incentive leakage to business value;
- show technical agents only after the business problem was clear;
- make a POC feel low-risk and concrete;
- avoid invented ROI/time claims;
- close on commitment and defensible outcomes.

## Major Narrative Turns

1. The opening moved away from generic AI promise toward "full-file review to exception review."
2. The challenge slide was revised around Robin's own words: PMs spend 1+ hour reviewing AI output and repeat this a few times a week.
3. A new fraud slide was inserted after the sample review to explain why the adoption gap exists:
   - bad actors chase incentives;
   - impersonation of construction/home-improvement pros matters;
   - speeders, straightliners, duplicates, and brand inconsistencies are easier than open-end fraud;
   - AI-written open-ends defeat the customer's most trusted filter.
4. Static GPT was framed as annotating, while agents decide.
5. "What to Expect" was reframed as recurring value, not a one-time cleanup.
6. Success metrics were sharpened around adoption and client defensibility.
7. The technical slide was inserted later in the deck and then visually normalized.
8. The deck was given a faint white/gray grid background and a final thank-you slide.

## Language That Landed

Use these lines or variants:

- "Step 5 is the cost."
- "Annotation assistant, not trusted decision support."
- "The adoption gap exists because the hardest fraud now looks plausible."
- "Static GPT annotates; agents decide."
- "Catch more real fraud, trust the call, and review only what needs a human."
- "The payout has three parts we will size with you in Discovery."
- "Internally: less operational effort and a workflow every PM actually adopts."
- "Externally: data quality you can stand behind as a differentiator - not just clean data, but defensible data."

## Meeting Strategy Lessons From Claude Thread

The meeting should not be a canned pitch. Open with 5-10 minutes of discovery, then tailor the presentation emphasis.

Recommended flow:

- Morgan opens, guides key questions, owns business value and the close.
- Jeremy leads the deck and technical questions.
- One person not leading should capture SOW inputs.
- Joe, if present, acts as a positioning guardrail, not as a third presenter.

The live artifact should be a one-page operational sheet, not a long script. Include:

- validated numbers;
- do-not-say numbers;
- audience read (Robin versus Dave);
- note-taking assignment;
- contingencies;
- close language.

## Audience Read

Robin built the existing GPT. Do not make her feel like it was a bad idea. The message is: she proved there is signal; the POC turns that signal into a trusted workflow.

Dave is likely the business-case audience. Aim value at incentive leakage, PM labor, and client trust.

## Contingency Branches

- If they ask why not prompt it better: a prompt can annotate, but adoption requires evidence, routing, override learning, and auditability.
- If they ask for a free pilot: the POC requires real workflow build and calibration on real exports.
- If they want to skip the pilot: keep the pilot as the fastest path to scope the full build safely.
- If the decision maker is absent: secure a SOW-review meeting, not a vague follow-up.
- If batches are small: scope around frequency, cumulative overhead, and fraud-risk exposure, not one batch alone.

## Visual Evolution

The final deck style moved toward:

- bright white background;
- faint low-opacity gray hairline grid;
- small uppercase header labels;
- right-aligned confidential/page marker;
- black headlines and white rectangular cards;
- minimal visible source notes;
- dense technical slide only after business framing.

The grid should be real hairline connectors, not filled rectangles, to avoid intersection artifacts.
