# Deck DNA

## Narrative Posture

This deck is a business-positioning deck first and a technical deck second. It should feel like a senior consultant translating an agentic AI build into a concrete operating change.

The central thesis:

- The customer does not need a better prompt.
- The customer needs a trusted, repeatable, auditable workflow.
- The workflow should review everything, route exceptions, attach evidence, and learn from overrides.

## Tone

Use calm, precise, executive language. Avoid hype. The deck should sound confident because it is grounded in the customer's own process and files.

Preferred phrases:

- "Exception review, not full-file review."
- "The model is not the differentiator; the calibrated workflow around it is."
- "PMs carry the trust burden today."
- "Static GPT annotates; agents decide."
- "Bad respondents never reach a deliverable."
- "Data quality Farnsworth can stand behind with clients."
- "Light for the customer because the infrastructure runs on our side."
- "The pilot sizes the value with you."

Avoid:

- "AI magic", "autonomous everything", "just use agents", "platform revolution".
- Unsupported ROI precision.
- Language that implies the customer's GPT was foolish. It was a useful first attempt that never became trusted decision support.

## Structure And Pacing

Open with outcome and pain, not technology. Use the first third of the deck to establish the business gap. Put technology in the middle/back half after the audience understands why it matters.

The best rhythm:

1. Name the shift: full-file review -> exception review.
2. State the current bottleneck in the customer's words.
3. Show real file evidence.
4. Explain why fraud makes the gap hard.
5. Introduce agents as the operating model.
6. Show success measures and implementation choices.
7. Close on POC scope and commitment.

## Slide-Level Patterns

Use these patterns repeatedly:

- Big headline + tight support copy.
- Left/right contrast: problem versus agentic answer.
- Today/target table.
- Step row where each step ends with "so..." or "so that...".
- Bottom outcome strip for the single takeaway.
- Discovery slide with unanswered inputs, not fake certainty.

## Technical Content

Technology must be leashed to business outcomes. For every technical mechanism, add the operational result:

- "Agents cross-check identity, behavior, and open-ends -> so impersonation and AI-written answers get caught, not just speeders."
- "Evidence + confidence on every call -> so decisions are client-defensible."
- "PM overrides tune thresholds -> so each run gets sharper and cheaper."
- "Rules + open-end AI review every respondent -> so quality is checked, not sampled."

The technical overview slide can be dense, but it must still include a business-alignment line: agents should not simply flag more people; they should reduce missed bad respondents, explain why each exception matters, and keep legitimate respondents from being wrongly removed.

## Meeting-Driven Deck Logic

When the deck is used in a sales meeting, assume a 60-minute meeting:

- 2 minutes: welcome and purpose.
- 5-10 minutes: opening discovery before the deck.
- 2 minutes: presenter recalibrates what to emphasize.
- 20-25 minutes: tailored deck walkthrough.
- 20-30 minutes: scope discussion and POC commitment.

Roles from the Farnsworth pattern:

- Jeremy leads the presentation and technical/how questions.
- Morgan guides discovery, business questions, value, and the close.
- The non-leading person captures notes and SOW inputs.

Opening discovery should answer:

- Is the routine cleaning cycle the 50-200 interim batch, the full closeout file, or both?
- Where does the 1+ hour actually go?
- What does Farnsworth need to see to feel comfortable with a paid POC?

## Audience Read

For a Robin/Dave-style buyer pair:

- Robin built the current GPT. Respect the effort. Position the POC as adding workflow, evidence, calibration, and adoption around the signal she already proved was valuable.
- Dave likely needs the business case. Anchor to incentive leakage, PM labor, and client-trust risk.

If the decision maker is absent, the meeting floor outcome becomes a scheduled SOW review, not a same-meeting yes.

## Visual Grammar

Use a bright white background with a faint gray grid. Keep text black or dark gray. Use thin black rules and white cards. Keep cards rectangular. Avoid saturated backgrounds except for one technical decisioning block if needed.

Typography:

- Header label: 8-9 pt, uppercase, bold, Helvetica Neue.
- Page marker: 8 pt, regular, gray, right aligned.
- Main headline: 28-48 pt depending on slide.
- Body: 10-16 pt.
- Dense technical labels: 7-10 pt, only where necessary.

## Closing

End on outcomes, not AI:

"Internally: less operational effort and a workflow every PM actually adopts.

Externally: data quality you can stand behind as a differentiator - not just clean data, but defensible data."
